# Archived afterstate TD checkpoints

The afterstate TD experiments trained a value head on the 16 legal placements ranked highest by a frozen Spatial V4 actor. The head predicts additional successful placements after the chosen move, in thousands. A terminal afterstate has target zero. The archived training protocol records the original inputs, optimizer, calibration, time budget, checkpoint recovery, and screening procedure. [Original TD protocol](https://github.com/lisu188/tetris4d/blob/23159fe8d860a13ab16b8d5b69f2abf868522c82/docs/afterstate-td.md)

Pure TD checkpoints choose the highest predicted value. Actor-regularized checkpoints add a bounded value adjustment to each frozen actor logit. The adjustment is `clamp(beta * (value - highest_valid_value), -bound, 0)`. A zero bound reproduces the frozen actor’s greedy choices.

At update 40, the mean margin between the two highest predicted values on eight training boards was 0.45 placements. On four paired near-top-out boards capped at 1,500 additional placements, mean survival was 557.25 for bound 0.5, 196.5 for bound 1.0, and 220.25 for pure TD. The paired 95% interval for bound 0.5 relative to pure TD was −33 to +1,052 placements. In six passes over that rollout, moving the bootstrap head 1% toward the online head after each optimizer step reduced mean absolute target drift from 132 to 20 placements. The sample contained no episode endings.

`tools/evaluate_afterstate_td.py` can still compare saved TD, Spatial V4 actor, frozen-critic, and PPO checkpoints on paired seeds. Its report includes survival per seed, clears, placement caps, and paired bootstrap estimates. The original holdout protocol used a 64,000-placement cap and extended the leading pair to 128,000 if either had at least eight capped games.
